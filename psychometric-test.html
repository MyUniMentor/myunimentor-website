What the test measures

Six short parts. Interests tell you what you will enjoy, aptitude what comes easily to you, personality how you like to work, and values what you want from a job.

  1. 1

    Interests

    36 activities

    How much would you enjoy fixing a scooter, writing a script, running a stall or caring for patients? Scored on Holland’s six interest types (RIASEC), the model behind most career tests worldwide.

  2. 2

    Personality

    25 statements

    Social energy, warmth, discipline, calm under pressure and curiosity: the Big Five, measured with free research scales from the International Personality Item Pool.

  3. 3

    Aptitude

    6 timed sections

    Numbers, words, patterns, shapes you turn in your mind, gears and levers, and a one-minute speed check. About 12 minutes in all.

  4. 4

    Situations

    6 real moments

    Falling behind before boards, a teammate who isn’t pulling their weight, a hard problem you can’t crack. What you would really do.

  5. 5

    Values

    Rank 6

    Achievement, independence, recognition, relationships, support and working conditions: the six work values used by O*NET.

  6. 6

    About you

    1 minute

    Your class, stream, marks band, favourite subjects and how long you want to study, so every suggestion is one you can actually take.

How we match you to careers

Every career area has a profile. For interests, we use the U.S. Department of Labor’s O*NET interest codes for related jobs: software developers and data scientists are “Investigative and Conventional”, lawyers “Enterprising, Conventional and Investigative”, physiotherapists “Social, Investigative and Realistic”.

Your answers are compared with each profile and weighted like this:

  • 50% Interests
  • 25% Aptitude
  • 15% Personality
  • 10% Values

Subjects you enjoy add a little. If you are in Class 11 or 12 and a path needs a different stream, it is marked clearly, so you only plan around options open to you.

What we leave out, and why

No fingerprint (DMIT) tests. There is no scientific evidence that fingerprints predict talent or careers.

No “learning styles”. Reviews of the research have found no good evidence that teaching to a visual or auditory “style” helps. We give study tips with strong evidence instead, like testing yourself and spacing out revision.

No personality “types”. We measure traits on a scale, because people are rarely one type or the other.

Its limits, honestly

Each section is short, so treat scores as bands, not exact numbers. Aptitudes grow with practice. And for now we compare you with your own profile; comparisons with other students will be added once enough students have taken the test. That is why every report ends with a free session with a counsellor.

What is a psychometric test for career guidance?

A set of questions that measures your interests, abilities, personality and values, and then matches them to careers. MyUniMentor’s test takes about 25 minutes and matches you to 24 career areas, with the courses and entrance exams for each.

Who is it for?

Students in Class 9, 10, 11 and 12, and students taking a gap year. Class 9 and 10 students also see which stream each career path needs.

How much does it cost?

₹99, paid once. You can also unlock it free: share your invite link, and when three friends create free MyUniMentor accounts with it, your test unlocks. Schools and events may also give you an access code.

Can I stop and finish later?

Yes. Your answers save as you go to your free MyUniMentor account, so you can stop anytime and continue on any device. Each timed aptitude section keeps its remaining time.

How accurate is it?

It is a guidance tool, not a final answer. Interests are the strongest single guide to a career you will enjoy, which is why they carry half the weight. Each section is short, so read scores as rough bands, and talk your report through with a counsellor before deciding.

Can my parents see the report?

Yes, if you link your accounts. A parent who links to your account from their own MyUniMentor account can open your report once you finish.

Do you use DMIT or fingerprint tests?

No. Fingerprint-based tests (DMIT) are not backed by scientific evidence, and neither are “learning styles”. We leave both out.

How is it different from the free career check?

The free career check takes about 4 minutes and looks at your interests and plans. The psychometric test takes about 25 minutes and adds personality, six timed aptitude sections, real-life situations and values, with a much more detailed report.
