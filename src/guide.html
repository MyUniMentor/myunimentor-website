Before you begin

How to use this guide

Most students decide what to study after school on hearsay — a relative's advice, a coaching brochure, a friend's choice. This guide helps you replace guesswork with a simple process you can follow at home, with your parents.

Work through it in order. Each step takes 15–30 minutes. Keep a pen handy: some pages are worksheets.

The five steps
  1. Know yourself — interests, strengths and how you like to work
  2. Match your stream to course families
  3. Map the entrance exams those courses need
  4. Shortlist universities with a checklist, not a brochure
  5. Plan your Class 12 year month by month

This guide is general advice. Every student is different — your marks, budget, location and goals change the answer. For a plan made for you, book a free one-on-one session with MyUniMentor (T&C apply).

Step 1

Know yourself

Answer honestly. There are no right answers — only clues.

Which subjects do you enjoy, even when there's no exam?

What do people often ask you for help with?

Pick the three that sound most like you

  • I like solving problems with logic and numbers
  • I like building or fixing things
  • I like helping and caring for people
  • I like drawing, designing or making things look good
  • I like reading, writing and debating
  • I like leading, selling and organising
  • I like experiments and finding out why
  • I like working with computers and technology

What matters most to you in a career?

  • Stable income
  • Creativity
  • Helping society
  • Working abroad someday

Step 4

Shortlist universities with a checklist

Brochures show the best photo. A checklist shows the full picture. Tick each point for every university on your list.

  • Recognition: Is the university and the programme approved by UGC (and AICTE, NMC, BCI, COA or PCI where needed)?
  • Accreditation and rankings: What is its NAAC grade? Does it appear in NIRF?
  • Placements: What is the median package for your course — not just the highest?
  • Total cost: Tuition, hostel, mess, exam fees and other charges for all years.
  • Scholarships: What is available, and what marks or rank do you need?
  • Faculty: How many full-time faculty does your department have?
  • Internships: Are internships built into the course?
  • Campus life: Hostel quality, safety, clubs and facilities.
  • Location: Travel time and cost from home; cost of living in the city.
  • Alumni: Speak to at least one current student or graduate.

Step 4, continued

12 questions to ask a university before you apply

  1. Is this exact programme approved by the relevant regulator?
  2. What is the total fee for the full course, including hostel?
  3. What was the median placement package for this course last year?
  4. Which companies recruited from this course?
  5. How many students got placed, out of how many eligible?
  6. What scholarships can I get, and do they continue every year?
  7. Is there an internship or industry project in the course?
  8. How many full-time faculty teach in this department?
  9. What is the refund policy if I withdraw?
  10. Can I switch specialisation later?
  11. What support is there for higher studies or study abroad?
  12. Can I talk to a current student of this course?

Write the answers down. Compare at least three universities side by side before you pay any fee.

Step 5

Your Class 12 year, month by month

April–JuneFinish Steps 1 and 2. Pick two or three course families. List the entrance exams they need. July–AugustNote every registration window. Start exam preparation alongside school. Draft a first university list. September–OctoberApply for exams whose registration opens early (design and law exams often close in October–November). November–DecemberUse the Step 4 checklist on your university list. Visit campuses or attend career fairs if you can. January–FebruaryEngineering and design exams begin. Register for exams that open in the new year, like CUET and NEET. MarchBoard exams. Keep your exam plan on hold, but do not miss any registration deadline. April–JuneMost entrance exams and results. Apply to your final shortlist and track every counselling round. June–AugustCounselling, seat allotment and admission. Keep documents ready: marksheets, certificates, ID and photos.

Exact months vary by exam and year. Check the latest dates on our exam calendar at myunimentor.in/exam-calendar.
